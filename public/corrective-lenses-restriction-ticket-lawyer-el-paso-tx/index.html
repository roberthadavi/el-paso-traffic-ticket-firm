Corrective Lenses / License Restriction Ticket Lawyer El Paso, TX

Driving without corrective lenses (Code A) and other Texas license restriction violations under Transportation Code §521.461, including restriction removal at DPS.

Fight Your TicketCall 915-200-1133

A corrective lenses or license restriction ticket lawyer in El Paso, TX handles a citation that surprises many drivers: violation of driver's license restriction under Texas Transportation Code §521.461. The most common version is the Code A restriction, which requires corrective lenses, written on a driver who was wearing contact lenses the officer could not see, who had LASIK years ago, or whose vision improved and who simply never went back to DPS to update the license.

Because the citation is a Class C misdemeanor, paying it is a plea of guilty or no contest and creates a conviction on your record. In many cases there is a better path: updating or removing the restriction at the Texas Department of Public Safety and presenting that proof to the court. Attorney Robert Navar reviews the citation, your DPS record, and your vision documentation to determine whether the charge can be dismissed, deferred, or defended.

License Restrictions Under Texas Transportation Code §521.221

Section 521.221 authorizes DPS to place restrictions on a driver's license that it considers appropriate for the safe operation of a vehicle, based on the applicant's physical condition, driving experience, or the type of vehicle involved. The restriction prints on the license as a letter code and becomes a condition of the privilege to drive. Common Texas restriction codes include:

  • A — With corrective lenses (glasses or contacts).
  • B — A licensed operator age 21 or older must be in the front seat (learner license).
  • C — Daytime driving only.
  • D — Speed not to exceed 45 mph.
  • E — No expressway driving.
  • S — Outside rearview mirror or hearing aid required.
  • X — Automatic transmission only.
  • Ignition interlock — Required by a court order as a condition of bond, probation, or an occupational license; it appears as a separate restriction and carries its own, more serious, consequences if violated.

DPS publishes the full restriction and endorsement list on its driver license pages. Each letter is a separate restriction, and violating any of them is charged under the same statute.

The Offense: Violating a Restriction Under §521.461

Section 521.461 makes it an offense to violate a restriction or endorsement on your license. It is a misdemeanor punishable by a fine of up to $200, a Class C-level offense handled in El Paso Municipal Court, a County Justice of the Peace precinct, or one of the smaller municipal courts in El Paso County, depending on which agency wrote the ticket. Court costs are added to the fine.

The charge usually appears alongside the reason for the stop, such as speeding or a lane-change violation, because the officer notices the Code A restriction on the license and sees no glasses. Two points are worth understanding. First, the state must prove you were in fact driving without the required lenses, and contact lenses satisfy the restriction even when an officer cannot see them. Second, a restriction violation is a different offense from driving while license invalid under §521.457, which carries higher penalties and escalates to a Class B misdemeanor on repeat convictions. We make sure the charge actually filed is the one the facts support.

Removing the Corrective Lenses Restriction at DPS

If you no longer need corrective lenses, the restriction can be removed. DPS requires a vision screening showing you meet the standard for an unrestricted license without glasses or contacts; the general standard is 20/40 or better in at least one eye, with an adequate field of vision. The process is straightforward:

  • Visit a DPS driver license office in El Paso and ask to have Restriction A removed.
  • Pass the in-office vision screening without lenses, or bring a completed vision examination report from an optometrist or ophthalmologist on the DPS form.
  • Pay the fee for a replacement license reflecting the change.
  • Keep the receipt and the temporary license; they are the proof we present to the court.

Drivers who had LASIK or cataract surgery should obtain a post-operative letter from the surgeon confirming the result. Drivers who wear contacts and want to keep the restriction should obtain a current prescription or a letter from their eye-care provider showing contact lenses were prescribed on or before the citation date.

Dismissal With Proof and Other Resolutions

Unlike an expired-registration ticket, there is no statute that requires a court to dismiss a restriction violation on proof of correction. In practice, El Paso courts and prosecutors regularly consider dismissing a first-time Code A citation when the driver shows either that the restriction has been removed by DPS or that the driver was wearing contact lenses at the time. Our office presents that documentation at the first setting and requests dismissal where the facts support it.

Where dismissal is not offered, deferred disposition under Code of Criminal Procedure article 45.051 lets the court defer a finding of guilt, set conditions such as updating the license, and dismiss the case at the end of the period with no conviction. Deferred disposition is not available to commercial driver's license holders. A driving safety course is generally not the right tool for this charge because it is not a moving violation. If the officer's report does not support the charge, we set the case for trial.

Other Restriction Violations and Occupational Licenses

Not every restriction case is about eyeglasses. A learner-license holder cited for driving without a licensed adult in the front seat, a driver with a daytime-only restriction stopped after dark, or a driver on an occupational license who was outside the hours or purposes the court order allows will all face a §521.461 citation. Occupational license violations are particularly serious because the order can be revoked and the underlying suspension reinstated.

An ignition interlock restriction tied to a DWI bond or probation is a separate matter from the Class C citation. A violation can trigger bond revocation or a motion to revoke probation in the court that imposed the condition. If your restriction case involves an interlock or an occupational license, tell us when you call so that we coordinate with the court that issued the order.

Frequently asked questions

I was wearing contact lenses. Can I still be convicted of violating the corrective lenses restriction?

Contact lenses satisfy the Code A restriction. The state must prove you were driving without corrective lenses, and a current contact lens prescription or a letter from your eye-care provider is strong evidence that you were in compliance. Officers often write the citation because they cannot see contacts.

How do I get the corrective lenses restriction removed from my Texas license?

Visit a DPS driver license office, pass the vision screening without lenses or bring a vision examination report from an eye doctor on the DPS form, and pay the replacement license fee. The receipt and updated license are the proof presented to the court.

Will the court dismiss the ticket if I remove the restriction?

There is no statute that requires dismissal, but in many cases the court or prosecutor will consider dismissing a first-time citation when the restriction has been removed or the driver shows compliance. Deferred disposition is the usual alternative when dismissal is not offered.

What is the fine for violating a license restriction in Texas?

Transportation Code §521.461 sets a fine of up to $200, plus court costs. It is a Class C-level misdemeanor. A conviction is a criminal conviction on the court's record even though it is a fine-only offense.

I had LASIK years ago but my license still says Code A. Was I breaking the law?

Technically the restriction remains in effect until DPS removes it, so an officer can cite you. A letter from your surgeon and a prompt visit to DPS to remove the restriction usually resolve the case on favorable terms.
