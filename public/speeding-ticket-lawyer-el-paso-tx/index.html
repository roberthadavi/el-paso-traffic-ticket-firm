Speeding Ticket Lawyer El Paso, TX

We contest radar, lidar and pacing evidence and pursue dismissal or deferred disposition so a speeding ticket does not become a conviction on your record.

If you are looking for a speeding ticket lawyer in El Paso, TX, the most important thing to understand is that paying the ticket is not a neutral act. Under Texas law, paying a citation is a plea of guilty or no contest, and the result is a conviction on your driving record that insurers can see. Our office, led by attorney Robert Navar, defends speeding citations in El Paso Municipal Court, the El Paso County Justice of the Peace precincts, and the municipal courts in Socorro, Horizon City, Anthony, Vinton, Clint and Canutillo.

A speeding ticket is a Class C misdemeanor. It is a criminal charge, the State has the burden of proof, and the officer's speed reading is evidence that can be tested like any other evidence. In many cases there is a path to a dismissal, a reduction to a non-moving violation, or a deferred disposition that keeps the conviction off your record. What the State has to prove on a Texas speeding charge

Most El Paso speeding tickets are written under Transportation Code §545.351, which requires a driver to operate at a speed that is reasonable and prudent under the conditions, and §545.352, which sets the prima facie speed limits. The word prima facie matters. Driving over the posted limit is evidence that your speed was not reasonable and prudent, but it is not the end of the inquiry. The prosecutor must still prove, beyond a reasonable doubt, that you were the driver, that you were traveling at the speed alleged, and that the posted limit was lawfully established and properly signed on that stretch of road.

Each of those elements can be challenged. Speed limits altered by a city under §545.356 must be supported by an engineering study and ordinance, and the sign must be where the law requires it. A citation that gets the location, the limit or the statute wrong gives us something to work with.

How speed is measured, and what we examine

El Paso police, DPS troopers and county officers use three methods, and each has known failure points.

  • Radar. We request the unit's calibration and tuning-fork records, the officer's radar certification, and the patrol car's position. Radar measures the strongest reflection in its beam, not necessarily your car, so target identification on a busy stretch of I-10 or Mesa Street is a live issue. Moving-mode radar also depends on the patrol speed being correctly computed.
  • Lidar (laser). Lidar is more precise than radar when used correctly, but it depends on a steady aim at a single vehicle over a fraction of a second. Sweep error, aiming at a reflective surface other than the plate or headlight, and use through a windshield or at long range are all points we examine.
  • Pacing. When an officer follows you and reads the patrol car's speedometer, the questions are the distance paced, whether a constant following distance was actually maintained, and when the speedometer was last calibrated.

We also review the citation itself, the officer's notes, and any dash-camera or body-camera footage. An officer who cannot appear, or whose records do not support the reading, cannot prove the case.

What a speeding conviction costs beyond the fine

The court fine for a Class C speeding offense is capped at $200 plus court costs, but the fine is the smallest part of the cost. A conviction goes on your Texas driving record, and auto insurers in Texas review that record at renewal. A single moving-violation conviction can raise premiums for three years or more, and a second one compounds it. For drivers who are young, drive for work, or have a prior conviction, the insurance cost usually exceeds the fine many times over.

Texas repealed the Driver Responsibility Program surcharges on September 1, 2019, so there are no longer state surcharges tied to points. However, the Department of Public Safety may still move to suspend a license for repeated moving-violation convictions, generally four or more within 12 months or seven within 24 months. If you already have convictions on your record, keeping this one off matters more, not less.

Driving safety course dismissal: who qualifies and who does not

Texas lets many first-time defendants complete a TDLR-approved driving safety course (often called defensive driving) in exchange for dismissal. The court generally must grant the request if you hold a valid Texas driver's license, have liability insurance, have not completed a course for a ticket in the last 12 months, and request it on or before your appearance date. The course route is explained in more detail on our defensive driving in El Paso page.

The course is not available in every case. By statute it does not apply if you were cited for 25 miles per hour or more over the limit, or for 95 miles per hour or more, regardless of the posted limit. It is not available to anyone who holds, or held at the time, a commercial driver's license. School crossing zone offenses are also excluded from the statutory right. And because it can be used only once in 12 months, it is sometimes better to save it. Part of our job is deciding whether the course, a deferred disposition, or a contested hearing is the right tool for your ticket.

Deferred disposition and other outcomes we pursue

Deferred disposition under Code of Criminal Procedure art. 45A.301 (formerly art. 45.051) lets the judge defer a finding of guilt for up to 180 days. You pay a special expense fee, comply with any conditions, and avoid new citations during the period; the case is then dismissed and no conviction is reported. Deferred disposition is not available to CDL holders, and judges in El Paso have discretion to deny it for very high speeds or for drivers with a recent history. We prepare the request so the court has a reason to grant it.

Where the evidence is weak, we will pursue dismissal outright. Where it is not, we may negotiate a reduction to a non-moving violation such as a defective equipment charge, which carries a fine but does not appear as a moving violation to insurers. The right outcome depends on your speed, your record, your license type and the court. Read more about the process on our fight your ticket page.

Frequently asked questions

Should I just pay my El Paso speeding ticket?

Paying is a plea of guilty or no contest and results in a conviction on your driving record. Before paying, find out whether you qualify for a driving safety course, deferred disposition, or a contested hearing. In many cases one of those keeps the conviction off your record for less than the long-term insurance cost of paying.

Can I take defensive driving for a speeding ticket in Texas?

Often yes, if you have a valid Texas license, liability insurance, have not used the course in the last 12 months, and ask before your appearance date. The course is not available for 25 or more over the limit, for 95 mph or more, for CDL holders, or for school crossing zone offenses.

How much is a speeding ticket in El Paso?

A Class C speeding offense carries a fine of up to $200 plus court costs, and the amount usually scales with how far over the limit you were. The larger cost is typically the insurance increase that follows a conviction.

Can radar or lidar evidence really be challenged?

Yes. The State must show the device was properly calibrated and operated by a trained officer and that the reading was of your vehicle. Missing calibration logs, lapsed certifications, poor target identification and an officer who does not appear are all real issues in Class C cases.

What happens if I ignore the ticket?

The court can issue a warrant, add a Failure to Appear charge, and place an OmniBase hold that blocks your driver's license renewal. If that has already happened, contact us; we handle warrant lifting as part of the ticket defense.

Do I have to go to court if I hire your office?

For most settings in El Paso Municipal Court and the Justice of the Peace courts, our office can appear on your behalf. If the case proceeds to trial, you may need to be present, and we will tell you in advance.
