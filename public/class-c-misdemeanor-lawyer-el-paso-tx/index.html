Class C Misdemeanor Lawyer El Paso, TX

Fine-only offenses like public intoxication, disorderly conduct, theft under $100 and assault by contact are still criminal convictions; we work to keep them off your record.

Fight Your TicketCall 915-200-1133

A Class C misdemeanor lawyer in El Paso handles the lowest level of criminal offense in Texas, and the one most people underestimate. Under Penal Code §12.23, a Class C misdemeanor is punishable by a fine of up to $500 and no jail time. That sounds minor until you learn that it is still a criminal conviction, that it appears on background checks, and that the "no jail" rule has an exception: if you do not pay the fine, the court can issue a capias pro fine and you can be arrested.

Traffic tickets are the most common Class C cases, but El Paso Municipal Court and the County Justice of the Peace courts also handle public intoxication, disorderly conduct, shoplifting under $100, minor in possession, open container, drug paraphernalia and assault by contact. Attorney Robert Navar and our office defend all of them with the same goal: resolve the case in a way that does not leave a conviction on your record, and where the law allows, get the record expunged.

What makes an offense Class C

Texas divides misdemeanors into Class A, B and C. Class A and B carry possible jail time and are filed in county court. Class C is fine-only, with a maximum of $500 under Penal Code §12.23, and is filed in municipal court or before a Justice of the Peace. For most Transportation Code violations the ceiling is lower, $200 under §542.401, but the Penal Code offenses discussed on this page carry the full $500 exposure plus state and local court costs that often exceed the fine itself.

Because there is no jail sentence, there is no right to a court-appointed attorney, and most defendants represent themselves or simply pay. That is the trap. Paying a Class C citation is a plea of guilty or no contest, the court enters a judgment of conviction, and the record is permanent.

Common non-traffic Class C offenses in El Paso

Beyond traffic, the Class C cases we see most often in the El Paso courts are:

  • Public intoxication, Penal Code §49.02: appearing in a public place while intoxicated to the degree that you may endanger yourself or another.
  • Disorderly conduct, Penal Code §42.01: abusive language, offensive gestures, fighting and similar conduct in public. Discharging or displaying a firearm raises it to Class B.
  • Theft under $100, Penal Code §31.03: shoplifting of low-value items is Class C, but it is still a theft conviction.
  • Minor in possession of alcohol, Alcoholic Beverage Code §106.05, and minor in possession of tobacco or e-cigarettes, Health and Safety Code §161.252.
  • Open container, Penal Code §49.031: an open alcoholic beverage in the passenger area of a vehicle on a public highway, whether or not anyone was drinking.
  • Possession of drug paraphernalia, Health and Safety Code §481.125: a Class C possession offense.
  • Assault by contact, Penal Code §22.01(a)(3): offensive or provocative physical contact without injury. Class C, but it is an assault record and can be a family-violence finding if the parties are family or household members.

Municipal court vs. Justice of the Peace court

El Paso Municipal Court has jurisdiction over Class C state offenses committed within the city limits and over city ordinance violations. Cases from El Paso Police officers, including PI and theft arrests downtown, go there. The El Paso County Justice of the Peace precincts have jurisdiction over Class C offenses throughout the county, and receive cases from DPS troopers, sheriff's deputies, constables and incidents outside the city. Socorro, Horizon City, Anthony, Vinton, Clint and Canutillo each have their own municipal courts for offenses inside their limits.

The substantive law is the same in every court, but procedures differ, and we tailor the approach to where the case is pending.

Deferred disposition: keeping the conviction off your record

Deferred disposition under Code of Criminal Procedure art. 45.051, now carried forward in Chapter 45A, is the main tool for resolving a Class C case without a conviction. The court defers a finding of guilt for up to 180 days, sets conditions such as a fee, a class or community service, and dismisses the charge if the conditions are met. The dismissal is not a conviction, and it is the gateway to expunction.

Eligibility is up to the judge, and some charges come with statutory conditions: alcohol-related offenses by minors typically require an alcohol awareness course, and the court may order counseling or restitution on theft or assault cases. CDL holders are not eligible for deferred disposition on traffic offenses, but that restriction does not apply to the non-traffic Class C offenses on this page. We prepare the request and make sure the conditions are realistic, because a violation of deferred terms results in a conviction.

Expunction of a Class C misdemeanor

A Class C conviction cannot be expunged. A Class C charge that was dismissed after deferred disposition can. Code of Criminal Procedure art. 55.01 (recodified as Chapter 55A) specifically allows expunction of a Class C misdemeanor when the person completed deferred disposition and the charge was dismissed. Expunction orders the court, the arresting agency and DPS to destroy the records, and once the order is signed you may lawfully deny the arrest and the charge in most situations.

An expunction is a separate civil case filed in district court. That is why the disposition in municipal or JP court matters so much: the difference between a $300 payment online and a deferred disposition with the same $300 in fees is the difference between a permanent record and a record that can be erased.

Why a Class C still shows up on background checks

Clients are often surprised to find a Class C on a background report years later. Court records in Texas are public. Commercial background-check vendors pull municipal and Justice of the Peace dockets, and a public intoxication or theft disposition shows up with the same weight as any other criminal case. If you were arrested and fingerprinted, as frequently happens on PI, assault by contact and theft, the arrest can appear in the DPS computerized criminal history as well.

Theft and assault convictions are the most damaging because employers, landlords, professional licensing boards and immigration authorities treat them differently from a traffic ticket. The practical answer is to avoid the conviction in the first place and then expunge the record, and that is the plan we pursue in every case where the law allows it.

Where the facts are weak we pursue dismissal or set the case for trial; Class C defendants are entitled to a jury trial, and the State still has to prove every element beyond a reasonable doubt. Frequently asked questions

Can you go to jail for a Class C misdemeanor in Texas?

Not as a sentence. Penal Code §12.23 limits the punishment to a fine of up to $500. However, if you fail to pay the fine after a conviction, the court can issue a capias pro fine and you can be arrested and held until the fine is satisfied or a hearing is held on alternatives. Police can also arrest you at the scene for many Class C offenses, such as public intoxication.

Is a Class C misdemeanor a criminal record?

Yes. A Class C conviction is a criminal conviction under Texas law. It appears in public court records that background-check companies search, and if you were arrested and fingerprinted it may appear in the DPS criminal history as well. A conviction cannot be expunged; a dismissal after deferred disposition can.

What is deferred disposition on a Class C case?

The court defers a finding of guilt for up to 180 days under Code of Criminal Procedure art. 45.051, sets conditions such as a fee or a class, and dismisses the charge when you complete them. The dismissal is not a conviction, and it makes you eligible to petition for expunction of the record.

Can a Class C theft charge be expunged?

If the charge is resolved without a conviction, for example by dismissal after deferred disposition, yes. Article 55.01 of the Code of Criminal Procedure allows expunction of a Class C charge dismissed after deferred disposition. If you pay the fine or are found guilty, the conviction is permanent.

Is my Class C case in municipal court or JP court?

If an El Paso police officer wrote the citation or made the arrest inside the city limits, the case is in El Paso Municipal Court. If a DPS trooper, sheriff's deputy or constable was involved, or the incident was outside the city, it is in a County Justice of the Peace precinct. The citation or release paperwork names the court, and we confirm it for every client.

Do I have to appear in court for a Class C misdemeanor?

In most Class C cases, an attorney can appear for you, and we handle the settings while you keep working. A judge can require your presence for certain hearings or for trial, and we will tell you in advance if that applies.
