CDL & Trucking Violation Lawyer El Paso, TX

CDL holders cannot use deferred disposition or a driving safety course, so every citation must be contested on the evidence. We defend I-10 and port-of-entry cases.

A CDL and trucking violation lawyer in El Paso, TX has to work under a different set of rules than a lawyer handling an ordinary ticket. Under 49 CFR 383.51 and the federal anti-masking regulation at 49 CFR 384.226, Texas courts may not defer, divert or otherwise mask a traffic conviction for a commercial driver's license holder. Deferred disposition and the driving safety course dismissal, the two tools that keep most Texas tickets off a record, are closed to you by statute. What is left is the evidence, and that is where our office, led by attorney Robert Navar, does its work.

We defend commercial drivers cited by El Paso police, DPS Commercial Vehicle Enforcement troopers, and county officers along the I-10 corridor, Loop 375, the Bridge of the Americas and Ysleta-Zaragoza ports of entry, and the industrial districts on the East side and in Socorro and Horizon City. Cases are heard in El Paso Municipal Court, the El Paso County Justice of the Peace precincts and the surrounding municipal courts. Why a CDL holder cannot just pay the ticket

For a non-commercial driver, a Class C ticket can usually be resolved with a driving safety course or a deferred disposition, and no conviction is reported. Texas law excludes CDL holders from both. Code of Criminal Procedure art. 45A.301 (formerly art. 45.051) states that deferred disposition does not apply to a motor vehicle offense committed by a person who holds, or held at the time, a commercial driver's license, and the driving safety course subchapter contains the same exclusion. These rules apply whether you were driving a tractor-trailer or your personal pickup on a Sunday.

Paying the ticket is a plea of guilty or no contest, the conviction is reported to DPS, DPS reports it to the Commercial Driver's License Information System, and your employer and insurer see it. Federal law also requires you to notify your employer within 30 days of any traffic conviction other than parking. A single paid speeding ticket can therefore be the first of the two convictions that trigger a disqualification. The only way to keep a conviction off a CDL record is to win, or to negotiate a result that is not a conviction for a moving violation.

Serious traffic violations and disqualification periods

The federal table at 49 CFR 383.51 defines the offenses that disqualify a commercial driver and for how long. The category that catches most of our clients is the serious traffic violation, which includes:

  • Speeding 15 miles per hour or more over the posted limit.
  • Reckless driving.
  • Improper or erratic lane changes.
  • Following too closely.
  • A traffic offense in connection with a fatal crash.
  • Driving a commercial motor vehicle without a CDL, without the CDL in your possession, or without the proper class or endorsement.
  • Texting or using a hand-held mobile phone while driving a commercial motor vehicle.

Two serious traffic violations within three years bring a 60-day disqualification; three bring 120 days. Violations committed in a personal vehicle count when they result in a suspension of your base license. Major offenses, including driving a CMV with a 0.04 blood-alcohol concentration or above, refusing a breath test, leaving the scene, or using a vehicle in a felony, carry a one-year disqualification for a first offense (three years with hazardous materials) and lifetime for a second. Railroad-crossing and out-of-service-order violations have their own escalating schedules. Texas enforces these through Transportation Code chapter 522. Logbook, hours-of-service, overweight and equipment citations

Not every commercial citation is a moving violation, but each one has its own consequence. Hours-of-service and electronic logging device violations under 49 CFR part 395 feed your carrier's CSA scores and your Pre-Employment Screening Program record, and a falsified-log finding can be career-ending. Overweight citations under Transportation Code chapter 621 carry fines that scale with the excess weight and are frequently written at the ports of entry and DPS inspection stations. Equipment violations under 49 CFR part 393, such as brake, tire, lighting and cargo-securement defects, are often the basis for an out-of-service order; a conviction for violating that order is a disqualifying offense under 383.51.

These cases turn on records: scale certifications, the inspection report, the ELD data, the bill of lading, and who is actually responsible for the defect, the driver or the carrier. We review the citation against the regulation it cites, because officers sometimes charge the driver for a carrier obligation or cite a state section that does not match the conduct described.

Phone use in a commercial vehicle

Using a hand-held mobile phone or texting while driving a commercial motor vehicle is both a federal serious traffic violation and a Texas offense. It is one of the most commonly written citations on I-10 through El Paso, and it is one where the facts are often thin: an officer sees a glow or a hand near the ear from a passing patrol car. Hands-free use with a single button press is permitted, and a driver who is lawfully stopped is not in violation. We request the officer's account in detail, dash-camera footage where it exists, and phone records where they help, because two of these convictions in three years mean 60 days without a license.

I-10, Loop 375 and the ports of entry

El Paso is one of the busiest commercial crossings on the southern border, and the traffic patterns create specific enforcement zones: the I-10 corridor from Anthony to the Hudspeth County line, Loop 375 (Cesar Chavez Border Highway and Joe Battle), the Bridge of the Americas and Ysleta-Zaragoza bridges, and the DPS inspection facilities serving them. Drivers based out of state or in Mexico are cited here constantly and cannot easily return for a court date.

Our office enters an appearance so an out-of-town driver does not have to be present for most settings, and so a missed date does not become a warrant with a Failure to Appear charge and an OmniBase hold on your license. If that has already happened, see our warrant lawyer page; a warrant can be lifted and the underlying ticket still contested.

How we defend a CDL citation

  • Identify immediately whether the charge is a serious traffic violation, a major offense, or a non-disqualifying citation, and what it would do to your record given your existing history.
  • Obtain the citation, officer's notes, video, calibration and inspection records, and ELD or scale data where relevant.
  • Contest the case at pretrial and, when the evidence is contestable, at trial. Because deferral is unavailable, we prepare every CDL case as if it will be tried.
  • Where the facts do not support a dismissal, negotiate toward a non-moving disposition that does not report as a serious traffic violation. We do not promise that result; we pursue it.

Frequently asked questions

Can a CDL holder take defensive driving or get deferred disposition in Texas?

No. Texas law excludes anyone who holds, or held at the time of the offense, a commercial driver's license from both the driving safety course dismissal and deferred disposition, even for a ticket received in a personal vehicle. This implements the federal anti-masking rule at 49 CFR 384.226.

I got a speeding ticket in my own car. Does it affect my CDL?

Yes. Convictions in a personal vehicle are reported on your CDL record. Speeding 15 or more over the limit is a serious traffic violation, and two serious traffic violations within three years that affect your license result in a 60-day disqualification.

What counts as a serious traffic violation?

Under 49 CFR 383.51: speeding 15 mph or more over, reckless driving, improper lane change, following too closely, a traffic offense connected to a fatal crash, driving a CMV without a proper CDL or endorsement, and texting or hand-held phone use in a CMV. Texas applies the same list through Transportation Code chapter 522.

Do I have to tell my employer about a ticket?

Federal regulations require a CDL holder to notify the employer within 30 days of any conviction for a traffic violation other than parking, in any type of vehicle. A conviction, not a citation, triggers the duty, which is one more reason to resolve the citation without a conviction where possible.

I live out of state. Do I have to come back to El Paso for court?

In most cases our office can appear for you at pretrial settings in El Paso Municipal Court and the Justice of the Peace courts. If the case goes to trial, you may need to be present, and we will schedule that with you in advance.

What happens if I ignore a ticket I got passing through El Paso?

The court issues a warrant and can add a Failure to Appear charge and an OmniBase hold, which blocks renewal of your license in Texas and is reported to your home state. For a CDL holder that can mean an immediate loss of work. Contact us before that happens, or as soon as it has.
