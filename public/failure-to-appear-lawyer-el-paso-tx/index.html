Failure to Appear Lawyer El Paso, TX

A missed court date adds a separate Failure to Appear charge, a warrant and license and registration holds; we defend the FTA charge and clear the holds.

Fight Your TicketCall 915-200-1133

A failure to appear lawyer in El Paso handles a problem that is bigger than the original ticket. When you miss an appearance date on a Class C citation, the court does not simply wait. It issues a warrant, files a new criminal charge for the missed appearance itself, and reports you to a state program that blocks your Texas driver's license renewal and, in many cases, your vehicle registration. One missed date can turn a $150 speeding ticket into two cases, several hundred dollars in costs and a license you cannot renew.

Our office, led by attorney Robert Navar, untangles all of it. We post the appearance bond that recalls the warrant, defend the separate Failure to Appear charge, and get the OmniBase and scofflaw holds released once the cases are resolved. If your main concern is the warrant itself, start with our warrant lawyer page. This page explains the Failure to Appear charge and the holds that come with it.

The Failure to Appear charge is a separate offense

Two Texas statutes create a stand-alone offense for missing court on a ticket:

  • Transportation Code §543.009 — When you signed the citation, you made a written promise to appear. Breaking that promise is a misdemeanor "regardless of the disposition of the charge on which the person was arrested." In other words, even if the original ticket is later dismissed, the Violation of Promise to Appear (VPTA) charge can stand on its own.
  • Penal Code §38.10 — Bail jumping and failure to appear. When the underlying case is punishable by fine only, the Failure to Appear offense is a Class C misdemeanor carrying a fine of up to $500 under Penal Code §12.23.

El Paso Municipal Court and the County Justice of the Peace courts routinely file one of these charges when an appearance date passes. The practical result is that a driver who missed one ticket now has two cases, each with its own fine and court costs, and both must be addressed before the holds come off.

From missed date to alias warrant to capias pro fine

The paperwork escalates in a predictable sequence, and the stage you are in determines what we can do:

  1. Appearance date missed. The court sends a notice, and after the grace period it issues an alias warrant. This is a pre-judgment warrant. You have not been convicted of anything yet, which means the original ticket can still be fought.
  2. Failure to Appear charge filed. A second complaint under §543.009 or §38.10 is added, usually with its own alias warrant.
  3. Judgment entered and fine unpaid. If a conviction has already been entered, by plea, by a trial you did not attend, or by a payment plan you fell behind on, the court issues a capias pro fine. This is a post-judgment warrant to collect the fine, and it is a different animal: the issue is no longer guilt but how the fine will be satisfied.

Many drivers do not know which stage they are in. We check every client's name in both the municipal and county systems through our ticket and warrant lookup before recommending a plan.

Recalling the warrant with an appearance bond

For an alias warrant on a Class C case, the Code of Criminal Procedure allows an attorney to post an appearance bond and ask the court to recall the warrant and set the case on the docket. Once the bond is accepted, the warrant is lifted, the case returns to pretrial status, and you are no longer at risk of arrest at a traffic stop or at the port of entry. We handle the filing, and you do not have to go to the court or the jail.

With the warrant recalled, we treat the original ticket and the Failure to Appear charge as two cases to be defended together. Where the facts support it, we ask the prosecutor to dismiss the FTA charge as part of resolving the underlying ticket, or we present the reasonable-excuse defense the Penal Code recognizes when the missed appearance was caused by circumstances beyond your control. We do not promise that the FTA charge will be dropped, but in our experience courts are far more receptive to a driver who comes forward through counsel than to one who is brought in on the warrant.

OmniBase: the driver's license renewal hold

Chapter 706 of the Transportation Code created the Failure to Appear program, administered by a private contractor and commonly called OmniBase. When a court reports a missed appearance or an unpaid fine, the Department of Public Safety is prohibited from renewing your Texas driver's license until the court clears the report. Your license does not get suspended; it simply cannot be renewed when it expires, and you will not be able to get a replacement or a change-of-address card.

The hold is released only after the court is satisfied, by a dismissal, a completed disposition or full payment, and the court submits the clearance. A $10 administrative fee under §706.006 is charged for each reported case. If you have several old tickets, each one carries its own fee and its own clearance. We obtain the clearance paperwork as part of closing each case and confirm with DPS that the hold is gone before we consider the matter finished.

Scofflaw: the vehicle registration hold

Transportation Code §502.010 lets a county tax assessor-collector refuse to register a vehicle when the owner has an outstanding warrant for failure to appear or failure to pay a fine on a Transportation Code complaint, or on any offense in a municipal court that has contracted with the county. El Paso County participates, so a warrant from El Paso Municipal Court or a Justice of the Peace can block your registration renewal at the county tax office and through the online portal. A $20 fee is added when the hold is released.

Driving on expired registration then produces another citation, which produces another appearance date, and the cycle repeats. The only way out is to clear the underlying warrants. We address the scofflaw hold at the same time as the OmniBase hold so both come off together.

How we resolve a Failure to Appear case

A typical engagement looks like this:

  • Confirm every open case, warrant and hold in El Paso Municipal Court, the County JP precincts and the surrounding municipal courts in Socorro, Horizon City, Anthony, Vinton, Clint and Canutillo.
  • Post appearance bonds on alias warrants and ask the court to set the cases for pretrial.
  • Defend the original ticket on its merits, pursuing dismissal, deferred disposition or a driving safety course where you are eligible.
  • Address the Failure to Appear charge, seeking dismissal where the facts support it and minimizing the result where they do not.
  • On a capias pro fine, request a hearing on alternatives such as a payment plan, community service or a reduction based on inability to pay, which the Code of Criminal Procedure requires the court to consider.
  • Obtain OmniBase and scofflaw clearances and verify them with DPS and the county.

Frequently asked questions

Is Failure to Appear a crime in Texas?

Yes. Violating the written promise to appear on a citation is a misdemeanor under Transportation Code §543.009, and Penal Code §38.10 makes failure to appear a Class C misdemeanor when the underlying case is fine-only. It is charged separately from the original ticket and carries its own fine and court costs.

If my original ticket is dismissed, does the Failure to Appear charge go away too?

Not automatically. Section 543.009 says the offense exists regardless of how the original charge is resolved. In practice we ask the prosecutor to dismiss the FTA charge as part of a global resolution, and courts often agree when the driver has come forward through counsel, but it has to be addressed on its own.

What is the difference between an alias warrant and a capias pro fine?

An alias warrant is issued before any judgment, when you miss an appearance; the ticket can still be fought. A capias pro fine is issued after a conviction when the fine has not been paid; the question is how the fine will be satisfied, and the court must consider alternatives such as payment plans or community service.

Why will DPS not renew my license?

The court reported you to the Failure to Appear program under Transportation Code chapter 706, commonly called OmniBase. DPS cannot renew your license until the court clears the report, which happens only after the case is resolved. A $10 fee applies to each reported case.

Can I just pay the old tickets to clear the warrant?

You can, but paying is a plea of guilty or no contest and produces a conviction on each ticket, plus a conviction on the Failure to Appear charge. If the warrants are alias warrants, an attorney can post bonds, recall the warrants and defend the cases instead, often with a better outcome on your driving record.

Do I need to go to the court or the jail to lift the warrant?

Usually not. On a Class C alias warrant, our office posts the appearance bond and files the recall request. You stay home and we handle the court settings. We will tell you if your particular case requires a personal appearance.
