Texas State Trooper Ticket Lawyer El Paso, TX

Highway Patrol tickets written on I-10, US-54, Loop 375 and Hwy 20 are filed in El Paso County Justice of the Peace courts, and we handle them without you returning to El Paso.

Fight Your TicketCall 915-200-1133

If a Texas Department of Public Safety trooper stopped you on I-10, US-54, Loop 375 or Highway 20, you need a state trooper ticket lawyer in El Paso who knows where that citation actually goes. Unlike a ticket from the El Paso Police Department, a DPS Highway Patrol citation is not filed in El Paso Municipal Court. It is filed in one of the El Paso County Justice of the Peace precincts, each with its own judge, clerk, docket and procedures.

Our office, led by attorney Robert Navar, appears in the El Paso County JP courts on trooper tickets for local drivers, Fort Bliss soldiers and out-of-town motorists who were simply passing through on the way to Las Cruces, San Antonio or Juárez. In most Class C cases we can enter an appearance, request a court setting and resolve the matter without you ever coming back to El Paso County.

Where DPS trooper tickets are filed in El Paso County

Texas Highway Patrol troopers patrol the state highways and the unincorporated parts of the county. Their citations are filed with the Justice of the Peace precinct that covers the location of the stop. In practice that means:

  • I-10 West and the Upper Valley / Anthony / Canutillo corridor — typically a westside JP precinct.
  • I-10 East toward Horizon City, Clint and Fabens — typically an eastside or Lower Valley precinct.
  • US-54 (Patriot Freeway) and the Northeast — the precinct covering the Fort Bliss and Northeast area.
  • Loop 375 and Highway 20 (Alameda / Doniphan) — depends on the segment where the trooper made the stop.

The precinct number is usually printed on the citation. If it is not, the county's Odyssey public access portal at casesearch.epcounty.com lets you search by name once the trooper's paperwork is entered, which can take a few weeks. We also check every new client's name through our ticket and warrant lookup so nothing is missed.

Trooper tickets vs. city police tickets

The offense is the same under the Texas Transportation Code whether a trooper or an El Paso police officer writes it, and both are Class C misdemeanors with a fine of up to $200 for most moving violations. The difference is the court and the officer:

  • Court. City police tickets go to El Paso Municipal Court. DPS, sheriff and constable tickets go to a county Justice of the Peace. Each JP sets its own docket and its own practices on driving safety course and deferred disposition requests.
  • Radar and documentation. Troopers generally work with in-car video, radar or lidar, and detailed stop notes. We request that evidence and review it before advising you.
  • Officer availability. A trooper assigned to a highway sector may be reassigned or on duty elsewhere on a trial date. We track every setting and evaluate the case if the State cannot present its witness.

Whichever court your case lands in, the first rule is the same: do not simply pay the ticket. In Texas, paying is a plea of guilty or no contest and results in a conviction on your driving record.

Common Highway Patrol citations we defend

Trooper citations on the El Paso highways most often involve:

  • Speeding under §545.351, including 80-plus mph on the 75 mph stretches of I-10 east of the city.
  • Failure to maintain financial responsibility (no insurance) under §601.191.
  • No driver's license or expired license, and failure to display a license.
  • Expired registration, defective equipment and window tint.
  • Move Over / Slow Down violations under §545.157 near stopped emergency vehicles.
  • Commercial vehicle violations from DPS commercial enforcement at the weigh stations and ports of entry.

Each has its own defenses and its own consequences. A speeding ticket may be eligible for a driving safety course; a CDL holder's ticket is not. We explain the options that actually apply to your case rather than a one-size-fits-all answer.

Out-of-town drivers and Fort Bliss soldiers

A large share of trooper tickets near El Paso are written to people who do not live here: travelers on I-10, truck drivers, winter visitors, and service members stationed at Fort Bliss or passing through on orders. You have two practical problems. First, a court date in El Paso County may be hundreds or thousands of miles from home. Second, the conviction does not stay in Texas. Through the Driver License Compact, a Texas conviction is reported to your home state, which applies its own points and insurance consequences.

Under the Code of Criminal Procedure, a defendant in a fine-only misdemeanor may generally appear through counsel. That is the service we provide. Once you retain our office, we file a letter of representation and plea of not guilty, request the court's docket settings, and communicate with the prosecutor and clerk on your behalf. Fort Bliss soldiers with out-of-state licenses, deployments or PCS orders should tell us immediately so we can document the schedule for the court.

How we handle a trooper ticket without you returning

Our process for a DPS citation in the El Paso County JP courts:

  1. Locate the case. We confirm the precinct, cause number and appearance deadline so the ticket does not go into warrant status.
  2. Enter an appearance. We file the not guilty plea and request a pretrial setting. From that point the court's notices come to our office.
  3. Review the evidence. We request the trooper's report, video and radar or lidar documentation and look for problems with the stop, the speed measurement or the citation itself.
  4. Negotiate or try the case. Depending on the facts, we may pursue a dismissal, a deferred disposition that keeps the conviction off your record if you complete the conditions, a driving safety course where you are eligible, or a reduction to a non-moving violation. If none of those is appropriate, we set the case for trial.

We cannot promise a particular result, and no lawyer honestly can. What we can promise is that your case will be reviewed by a Texas attorney, every deadline will be tracked, and you will know your options before anything is decided. Read more about the process on our fight your ticket page.

What happens if you ignore a trooper ticket

Drivers who live out of state sometimes assume an El Paso County ticket will fade away. It will not. When the appearance date passes, the Justice of the Peace can issue a warrant and add a separate failure to appear charge. The court can then report you to the OmniBase program, which blocks renewal of a Texas driver's license, and many home states will refuse to renew your license until the Texas matter is cleared. The original $200 ticket becomes two cases, two sets of court costs and an active warrant that follows you to your next traffic stop anywhere in Texas. If that has already happened, our warrant lawyer page explains how an appearance bond can lift the warrant and restart the case.

Frequently asked questions

I got a ticket from a DPS trooper on I-10 in El Paso. Which court is it in?

DPS Highway Patrol tickets in El Paso County are filed in a Justice of the Peace precinct, not El Paso Municipal Court. The precinct is usually printed on the citation, and the case can be searched on the county's Odyssey portal at casesearch.epcounty.com once the clerk enters it. We confirm the court for every client before the appearance deadline.

Do I have to come back to El Paso for court?

In most Class C cases, no. Texas law allows a defendant in a fine-only misdemeanor to appear through an attorney. Once you hire our office we enter an appearance, receive the court's notices and handle the settings. If a trial is set and your testimony is needed, we discuss that with you well in advance.

Will a Texas trooper ticket show up on my out-of-state license?

If the ticket results in a conviction, Texas reports it under the Driver License Compact and your home state applies its own points or insurance consequences. That is why we work toward a disposition, such as deferred disposition or a driving safety course where eligible, that does not become a conviction.

Can I take defensive driving for a trooper ticket in a JP court?

Often yes, if you hold a non-commercial Texas license, were not more than 25 mph over the limit, have not used the course in the past 12 months, and had insurance at the time of the stop. Each Justice of the Peace applies the statute and its own local practice, and the request must be made before the appearance date.

I am stationed at Fort Bliss with an out-of-state license. Does that change anything?

Active-duty service members may lawfully drive in Texas on a valid home-state license. A trooper ticket is still filed in the El Paso County JP court and must be answered, but we regularly handle these cases around training, field exercises and PCS moves. Tell us your schedule and we will document it for the court.
