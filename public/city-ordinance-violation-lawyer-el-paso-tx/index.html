City Ordinance Violation Lawyer El Paso, TX

Code compliance, noise, junked vehicle, weeds, dumping, permit and business-license citations from the City of El Paso, resolved in Municipal Court before they become warrants.

Fight Your TicketCall 915-200-1133

A city ordinance violation lawyer in El Paso, TX handles a category of citation most people never expect to receive: a notice from Code Compliance, El Paso Police, the Fire Marshal, Parks and Recreation, or Environmental Services alleging that you violated the El Paso Municipal Code. High grass, a car on blocks in the driveway, a loud party, a shed built without a permit, or a dumped load of brush can each become a Class C misdemeanor complaint in El Paso Municipal Court.

These cases matter more than the citation suggests. Fines for health and safety ordinances can reach $2,000 per violation, each day a condition continues can be charged as a separate offense, and a citation that is ignored turns into a warrant and a hold on your driver's license. Attorney Robert Navar reviews the ordinance actually cited, the notice history, and the compliance timeline so that the case is resolved on the best available terms rather than paid as a conviction.

Ordinance Citations We Handle in El Paso

The El Paso Municipal Code is enforced by several departments, and the citation will name the title and section charged. The violations our office sees most often include:

  • Code compliance: weeds and high grass, accumulated trash or debris, inoperable or junked vehicles visible from the street, outdoor storage, and parking on an unimproved yard surface.
  • Noise: amplified music, loud parties, construction outside permitted hours, and vehicle exhaust or sound-system complaints.
  • Building and permits: construction, fences, carports, and additions built without a permit, work that does not pass inspection, and occupancy issues.
  • Illegal dumping and sanitation: dumping of refuse, brush, tires, or construction waste, and failure to maintain required trash service.
  • Business operations: operating without a required city license or certificate, sign violations, and vendor or mobile food permit issues.
  • Park and public-space rules: alcohol, hours, vehicles, and conduct rules in city parks and on city property.

If the citation came from a police officer rather than a code inspector, it will usually look like a traffic ticket and carry the same appearance deadline.

How Texas Law Treats a Municipal Ordinance Violation

A city ordinance violation is a Class C misdemeanor prosecuted by the City of El Paso in El Paso Municipal Court. Texas Local Government Code §54.001 caps the fine: up to $500 for most ordinances, up to $2,000 for an ordinance that governs fire safety, zoning, or public health and sanitation, and up to $4,000 for an ordinance that governs the dumping of refuse. Many of the code compliance citations in El Paso fall in the $2,000 tier because they are classified as health and sanitation rules.

Because the case is criminal, you have the same rights as a defendant on a traffic ticket: the right to plead not guilty, to see the complaint and the inspector's photographs and notes, to cross-examine the officer, and to a trial. The city must prove each element of the ordinance beyond a reasonable doubt, including that you are the owner, occupant, or person in control of the property on the date charged. Ownership disputes, tenant versus landlord responsibility, and defective notice are frequent issues in these cases.

Compliance-Based Dismissals and Abatement

Most El Paso code citations are written to force a property into compliance. Code Compliance usually issues a notice of violation with a correction deadline before a citation is filed, and the prosecutor will often consider dismissal or a reduced disposition once the condition has been abated. Our office documents the abatement so it carries weight at the setting:

  • Dated photographs of the property before and after the work.
  • Receipts for mowing, hauling, towing, or contractor work.
  • Permit applications, approved permits, and inspection sign-offs for building citations.
  • Proof that a vehicle was registered, repaired, moved into an enclosed structure, or removed.
  • Business license or certificate of occupancy obtained after the citation.

Where the city will not dismiss, deferred disposition under Code of Criminal Procedure article 45.051 allows the court to set conditions, often continued compliance for a period of months, and dismiss the case at the end without a conviction. Deferred disposition is available for most ordinance cases and is not limited by the driving safety course rules that apply to traffic tickets.

Why Resolve an Ordinance Citation Before It Becomes a Warrant

An ordinance citation carries an appearance date like any other Class C case. If that date passes with no plea and no appearance, the court can issue a warrant, add a separate failure-to-appear charge, and report the case to OmniBase, which prevents the Texas Department of Public Safety from renewing your driver's license until the case is cleared. The original $200 weeds citation can become several cases and well over a thousand dollars in fines and fees.

Continuing violations add to the exposure. Many El Paso ordinances allow each day that a condition remains to be charged as a separate offense, so an unresolved code case can generate new citations while the first one sits. A Note on Juvenile Curfew Citations

For many years El Paso enforced a juvenile curfew ordinance. In 2023 the Texas Legislature prohibited cities and counties from adopting or enforcing juvenile curfew ordinances, and those provisions are no longer enforceable. If a minor in your household received a citation that cites a curfew ordinance, or an old curfew case is still showing on the court's records, contact our office. Those cases should be dismissed, and a lingering warrant tied to one needs to be cleared.

Frequently asked questions

Is a code compliance citation in El Paso a criminal case?

Yes. Ordinance violations are Class C misdemeanors filed in El Paso Municipal Court. Paying the citation is a plea of guilty or no contest and results in a conviction on the court's record.

How much is the fine for a city ordinance violation in Texas?

Texas Local Government Code §54.001 allows a fine of up to $500 for most ordinances, up to $2,000 for fire safety, zoning, and public health and sanitation ordinances, and up to $4,000 for dumping of refuse. The actual fine is set by the court within those limits.

I fixed the problem. Will the ticket be dismissed?

In many cases the city will dismiss or reduce a citation once abatement is documented, but the decision is discretionary. Dated photographs, receipts, and permits presented before the case is set for trial make dismissal much more likely.

I am a tenant. Can I be cited for weeds or junked vehicles at a rental?

The ordinance usually reaches the owner, occupant, or person in control of the property. Who is responsible depends on the lease and the facts, and that is often a defense we raise for tenants and landlords alike.

Can the city cite me again while the first case is pending?

Yes. Many El Paso ordinances treat each day a condition continues as a separate offense, so abating the condition quickly limits the number of cases, even while we contest the original citation.
