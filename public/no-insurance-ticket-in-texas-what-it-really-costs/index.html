A No Insurance Ticket in Texas: What It Really Costs (and How to Beat It)

Published November 1, 2026 · by Attorney Robert Navar

A no insurance ticket is one of the most expensive citations an El Paso driver can receive, and almost none of the expense shows up on the face of the ticket. Our office handles failure to maintain financial responsibility cases in El Paso courts every week, and the first thing we tell a new client is that the fine is the smallest part of the problem. This post lays out the real cost of a conviction under Texas Transportation Code §601.191 and the ways we go about getting the charge dismissed.

What the statute actually requires

Texas Transportation Code Chapter 601 requires every driver to maintain "financial responsibility," which for nearly everyone means a liability insurance policy with at least the state minimum limits of $30,000 per person for bodily injury, $60,000 per accident, and $25,000 for property damage. Section 601.051 makes it an offense to operate a vehicle without that coverage, and §601.053 requires you to show evidence of coverage when an officer asks for it. An officer can accept a paper card, an electronic card on your phone, or a verification through the state database.

The offense is a Class C misdemeanor, which means it is handled in municipal or justice court and punishable by fine only. It is still a criminal conviction, and in Texas it is treated as a more serious one than most moving violations.

The fine: $175 to $350, then $350 to $1,000

Section 601.191 sets the fine range. For a first conviction the fine is not less than $175 and not more than $350. For a second or subsequent conviction the range jumps to not less than $350 and not more than $1,000. Court costs are added on top of the fine in every case. The old Driver Responsibility Program surcharge, which once added $250 per year for three years to a no insurance conviction, was repealed effective September 1, 2019, so that particular cost is gone. The rest of the consequences below are not.

SR-22 and suspension after a second conviction

This is where the cost multiplies. Under Transportation Code §601.231, when the Texas Department of Public Safety receives a second or subsequent conviction for failure to maintain financial responsibility, DPS suspends the driver's license and the registration of every vehicle the person owns. Section 601.232 keeps that suspension in place until the person proves to DPS that financial responsibility is in effect, and §601.233 requires the driver to maintain that proof for two years from the date of the conviction. If the proof lapses during those two years, the suspension returns.

"Proof" here means an SR-22 certificate, a form your insurance company files with DPS confirming that a policy exists and promising to notify DPS if it is cancelled. Insurers charge a filing fee for the SR-22, and most re-rate the driver as high risk for the duration. A driver with a prior conviction who receives a second ticket is facing a reinstatement fee, a two-year SR-22 obligation, and a suspended license and registration in the meantime. That is a far cry from a $350 fine.

The quiet cost: your insurance premium

Even a first conviction shows up on the driving record that insurers pull at every renewal. Companies treat a no insurance conviction as evidence of a coverage gap, and a coverage gap is one of the strongest rating factors in Texas auto insurance. Many of our clients report premium increases of several hundred dollars a year lasting three years or more, which dwarfs the fine. A dismissed case does not create a conviction and does not go on the driving record, which is why we fight these citations rather than advising clients to simply pay them.

How a no insurance ticket gets dismissed: §601.193

The Legislature built a dismissal mechanism directly into the statute. Transportation Code §601.193 provides that the court shall dismiss the charge if the defendant presents proof that a liability policy was actually in effect at the time of the offense. Note the exact wording: the policy must have been valid when you were stopped, not purchased afterward. If you had coverage and simply could not find the card, could not pull up the app, or the officer could not verify it, this section is the answer. We obtain a letter or declarations page from the insurer showing the effective dates and present it to the prosecutor and the court, and in these cases the charge is dismissed as a matter of law.

If the policy had genuinely lapsed on the day of the stop, §601.193 does not apply, and a different approach is needed, which we cover below.

TexasSure and why "unconfirmed" is not the same as "uninsured"

Texas operates a financial responsibility verification program under Subchapter N of Chapter 601, known as TexasSure. Insurers report policy data to the state, and officers can query the database from the patrol car using the license plate and VIN. When the database returns "unconfirmed," many officers write the ticket. In our experience the database is frequently wrong or behind. New policies and renewals can take weeks to appear. Policies in a spouse's or parent's name, commercial fleet policies, and out-of-state policies often never match correctly. An "unconfirmed" result is a reason for an officer to ask more questions; it is not proof of anything. When a client tells us the officer "ran it and it came back no insurance," we request the actual policy history from the carrier.

If there was a crash: impound and civil exposure

A no insurance ticket written at the scene of a collision is a different animal. El Paso police can and often do tow the uninsured vehicle, and storage fees at the lot accrue daily until the vehicle is released with proof of coverage and payment of the charges. More seriously, an uninsured at-fault driver is personally responsible for the other party's property damage and medical bills. A no insurance conviction in the traffic case can be used against you in that civil claim. We coordinate the handling of the citation with the client's exposure in the crash so that a plea in municipal court does not become an admission in a lawsuit.

Fort Bliss soldiers and out-of-state policies

A large share of our no insurance clients are service members stationed at Fort Bliss who carry a policy from their home state. That policy is valid in Texas as long as it meets Texas minimum limits, and most do. The difficulty is verification: an out-of-state policy will almost never appear in TexasSure, and officers working the Fort Bliss gates and the surrounding Northeast El Paso streets write a lot of these tickets. The fix is the same §601.193 dismissal, supported by a declarations page from the home-state insurer showing the policy was in force on the date of the stop. We also recommend that soldiers and their spouses keep a current digital card on their phones and a paper copy in the vehicle.

When the policy really had lapsed

If coverage had genuinely lapsed, our approach shifts to minimizing the conviction. The first step is getting insured immediately, before the court date, because judges and prosecutors in El Paso courts treat a driver who has fixed the problem differently from one who has not. From there, depending on the court and the driver's history, we pursue deferred disposition under Code of Criminal Procedure article 45.051, which results in a dismissal after a probationary period rather than a conviction, or a negotiated reduction. Deferred disposition is not available to commercial driver license holders, and the outcome depends on the facts, so we will not promise a result, but in many cases a lapsed-coverage ticket can still be kept off the driving record.

What to do if you were cited

Do not pay the ticket online. Paying is a plea of guilty or no contest and a conviction, and it forfeits the §601.193 dismissal even if you were insured. Gather your declarations page or a letter from your insurer showing the policy dates, note the court and the appearance date on the citation, and contact our office before that date. Frequently asked questions

I had insurance but could not find my card. Will the ticket be dismissed?

If a liability policy was actually in effect at the time of the stop, Transportation Code §601.193 directs the court to dismiss the charge once you present proof. We obtain the policy documentation from your insurer and present it to the court. The key is that the coverage must have existed on the date of the citation, not been purchased afterward.

Does a no insurance ticket suspend my license in Texas?

A first conviction does not automatically suspend your license. A second or subsequent conviction triggers a DPS suspension of your license and vehicle registration under §601.231, which continues until you file an SR-22 and maintain it for two years.

My out-of-state policy did not show up in the Texas database. Is that a defense?

Yes. An out-of-state policy that meets Texas minimum limits satisfies the financial responsibility requirement, and TexasSure rarely reflects out-of-state coverage. A declarations page from your home-state insurer showing the policy was in force on the date of the stop supports a §601.193 dismissal.

What if my coverage really had lapsed when I was stopped?

The §601.193 dismissal does not apply, but the case may still be resolved without a conviction. We recommend obtaining coverage immediately and then pursuing deferred disposition or a negotiated resolution, depending on the court and your record. Deferred disposition is not available to CDL holders.
