No Insurance Ticket Lawyer El Paso, TX

A §601.191 conviction means higher fines, then suspension and SR-22 on repeat. We pursue dismissal if you were covered and limit the damage if you were not.

If you need a no insurance ticket lawyer in El Paso, TX, start with this: a citation for failure to maintain financial responsibility under Transportation Code §601.191 is one of the few Class C tickets that gets worse every time it happens. The first conviction is a fine. The second brings a driver's license and registration suspension and a two-year SR-22 filing requirement. Many of the no-insurance tickets we see were written to drivers who were in fact covered: the card in the glovebox was expired, the policy was in a spouse's name, the car was borrowed, or the officer's database check was wrong. Texas law requires the court to dismiss the charge when the driver shows the policy was in effect at the time of the stop. If you were not covered, there are still ways to limit the damage. What §601.191 requires and what it costs

Chapter 601 of the Transportation Code, the Motor Vehicle Safety Responsibility Act, requires anyone operating a motor vehicle in Texas to establish financial responsibility, almost always through a liability policy with at least the state minimum limits (currently 30/60/25). Driving without it is a Class C misdemeanor under §601.191. The fine for a first conviction is not less than $175 and not more than $350, plus court costs. A subsequent conviction carries a fine of not less than $350 and not more than $1,000. The court may reduce a first-offense fine below $175 if it finds the defendant is unable to pay, but that does not remove the conviction.

The Driver Responsibility Program surcharges that used to add $250 a year for three years were repealed on September 1, 2019, so those no longer apply. The remaining consequences are the fine, the conviction on your driving record, the insurance-rate effect, and on a repeat offense the suspension and SR-22 described below.

Dismissal when you were actually insured

Section 601.193 makes it a defense to prosecution that the driver had financial responsibility in effect at the time of the offense, and it directs the court to dismiss the charge when the defendant presents satisfactory evidence of that coverage. In practice this means a declarations page or a letter from the insurer showing the policy period covered the date and time on the citation, and showing the vehicle or the driver was covered. An insurance card that has since expired, or that was printed after the stop, is not enough on its own; we obtain the right document from the carrier.

Officers in El Paso verify coverage through the TexasSure database, which pulls from insurer reporting that can lag by weeks after a new policy, a renewal or a vehicle change. A database miss is not proof of no insurance. We also see citations where coverage existed under a different household member's policy, which usually covers permissive drivers of the listed vehicle. Our first step on every §601.191 case is to find out whether a dismissal under §601.193 is available, because that result leaves nothing on your record.

Repeat offenses: suspension and SR-22

The serious consequences of a no-insurance conviction arrive on the second one. Under §601.231 and the sections that follow, the Department of Public Safety suspends the driver's license and vehicle registrations of a person convicted under §601.191 who has a prior conviction. The suspension continues until the person files proof of financial responsibility, and that proof must be maintained for two years from the date of the most recent conviction under §601.233.

Proof of financial responsibility in this context means an SR-22 certificate, which your insurer files with DPS and which signals to the market that you are a high-risk driver. Insurers charge more for SR-22 policies, and if the policy lapses during the two years the insurer must notify DPS and the suspension returns. Driving while suspended is itself a separate offense that can escalate to a Class B misdemeanor. If you have a prior §601.191 conviction, keeping a new charge from becoming a conviction is the entire objective.

Rental cars, borrowed cars and company vehicles

In Texas, liability coverage generally follows the vehicle, and most personal auto policies extend to anyone driving the car with the owner's permission. That means a borrowed car is usually covered by the owner's policy even if your name is not on it. Officers frequently cite the driver anyway because the card in the car is expired or the TexasSure lookup is keyed to the owner rather than the driver. The fix is to get the owner's declarations page and present it under §601.193.

Rental cars are covered by the rental company's self-insurance, by coverage you purchased at the counter, by your own personal auto policy if you have one, or by a credit-card benefit. Company vehicles are normally covered under the employer's commercial policy, and the officer may have no way to verify that at the roadside. In each case there is usually a document that resolves the charge; the challenge is knowing which one the court will accept and getting it in time for your appearance date.

Options when you were not insured

If there was no coverage at the time of the stop, the goal shifts from dismissal to keeping the conviction off your record. The usual tools are deferred disposition under Code of Criminal Procedure art. 45A.301 (formerly art. 45.051), which defers a finding of guilt while you obtain and maintain insurance and pay a special expense fee, and negotiating a reduction to a non-moving offense. Some El Paso courts are receptive to dismissal or deferral when a driver buys a policy promptly after the citation and brings proof; others require more. Deferred disposition is not available to commercial driver's license holders.

A no-insurance charge is not a moving violation, so a driving safety course dismissal does not apply to it directly. We review the whole citation, because a §601.191 charge is often written alongside a speeding or registration charge, and the overall strategy has to account for all of them. Frequently asked questions

I had insurance but could not find my card. Will the ticket be dismissed?

Under §601.193, the court is required to dismiss the charge when you show that a policy was in effect at the time of the stop. You will need documentation from the insurer covering the date, time and vehicle, not just a current card. We obtain and present that proof.

How much is a no insurance ticket in Texas?

A first conviction under §601.191 carries a fine of $175 to $350 plus court costs. A subsequent conviction carries $350 to $1,000, plus a license and registration suspension and a two-year SR-22 requirement.

What is an SR-22 and when do I need one?

An SR-22 is a certificate your insurer files with DPS proving you carry liability coverage. Texas requires it for two years after a repeat no-insurance conviction, and the license suspension stays in place until it is filed. It typically raises premiums significantly.

I was driving a friend's car. Whose insurance counts?

Most Texas personal auto policies cover permissive drivers of the insured vehicle, so the owner's policy usually satisfies the law. If the owner was insured on the date of the stop, that declarations page is generally the basis for a dismissal.

I bought insurance right after the ticket. Does that help?

It does not satisfy the §601.193 defense, which requires coverage at the time of the offense. It does support a request for deferred disposition or a negotiated non-conviction outcome, and some courts weigh it heavily. Bring the new policy to us before your appearance date.

Does a no insurance ticket affect my driving record?

A conviction is reported on your Texas driver record and is visible to insurers. It also counts as a prior for suspension and SR-22 purposes if you are ever cited again. Avoiding the conviction is the goal in every one of these cases.
