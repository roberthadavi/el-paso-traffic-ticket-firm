Moving Violation Lawyer El Paso, TX

Lane change, following too closely, failure to yield or signal, texting, seat belt, expired registration and inspection tickets in El Paso and El Paso County courts.

Fight Your TicketCall 915-200-1133

A moving violation lawyer in El Paso, TX handles the everyday traffic tickets that do not fit a single category: unsafe lane change, following too closely, failure to yield, failure to signal, texting while driving, seat belt, and the equipment and paperwork citations that ride along with a stop. Each of these is a Class C misdemeanor filed in El Paso Municipal Court, a County Justice of the Peace precinct, or one of the smaller municipal courts in Socorro, Horizon City, Anthony, Vinton, Clint, or Canutillo.

What makes these tickets worth contesting is the record they create. Paying the ticket is a plea of guilty or no contest, the conviction reports to your Texas driving record, and insurers read that record at every renewal. Attorney Robert Navar evaluates which charges can be dismissed outright, which qualify for a driving safety course or deferred disposition, and which should be set for trial.

Moving Violations We Handle in El Paso

The Texas Transportation Code defines most of these offenses in Chapter 545. The citations our office sees regularly include:

  • Unsafe lane change / failure to maintain a single lane — §545.060. The statute requires a driver to stay in a single lane as nearly as practical and to move only when it can be done safely, and the officer must be able to describe what made the movement unsafe.
  • Following too closely — §545.062. Common after a rear-end collision, where the citation is often written without the officer having seen the following distance.
  • Failure to yield right-of-way — §545.151 (intersections), §545.152 (turning left), and §545.153 (stop and yield signs). Fines increase substantially if the failure to yield caused a crash with injury.
  • Failure to signal — §545.104, which requires a signal for at least the last 100 feet before a turn or lane change.
  • Texting while driving — §545.4251, which prohibits reading, writing, or sending an electronic message while the vehicle is moving.
  • No seat belt — §545.413, including child-restraint charges for passengers under eight.
  • Expired registration — §502.407, and expired inspection — §548.602.

Speeding, red-light, and stop-sign tickets have their own pages: see our speeding ticket and red light and stop sign pages.

Moving vs. Non-Moving Violations: Why the Difference Matters

Texas does not use a single statutory definition, but in practice a moving violation is an offense committed while the vehicle is in motion: lane change, following distance, yield, signal, speeding, texting, and seat belt. A non-moving violation concerns the vehicle's condition or paperwork: expired registration, expired inspection, defective equipment, and most parking offenses.

The distinction drives two consequences. First, a moving-violation conviction appears on the driving record maintained by the Texas Department of Public Safety, and insurance carriers use that record to set rates. Second, moving violations count toward the license-suspension thresholds DPS applies to drivers with repeated convictions in a short period. Non-moving convictions generally do not affect insurance in the same way and are often dismissible on proof of correction. The Driver Responsibility Program and its surcharges were repealed on September 1, 2019, so there is no longer a state surcharge tied to points, but the driving record itself still matters.

Expired Registration and Inspection: Dismissal With Proof

Two of the most common non-moving citations have a built-in dismissal path. Under Transportation Code §502.407, a court must dismiss an expired-registration charge if the driver remedies the defect within 20 working days of the citation (or before the first court appearance, whichever is later) and pays an administrative fee not to exceed $20. Section 548.605 provides the same remedy for an expired inspection charge under §548.602.

Texas ended the annual safety inspection for most noncommercial vehicles on January 1, 2025, but El Paso County remains an emissions-testing county, so inspection-related citations still occur here, and the dismissal procedure still applies. The paperwork has to be presented correctly and on time, which is the step most drivers miss. Our office handles that filing so the charge is dismissed rather than paid.

Reckless Driving Is Not a Class C Ticket

One charge that sometimes gets grouped with moving violations deserves separate attention. Reckless driving under Transportation Code §545.401, driving with willful or wanton disregard for the safety of persons or property, is not a Class C misdemeanor. It is punishable by a fine of up to $200, confinement in county jail for up to 30 days, or both. Because jail is possible, the case is filed in a County Court at Law rather than Municipal Court, and a conviction creates a criminal record that follows you beyond the driving record.

Officers sometimes write reckless driving where the facts only support a lane-change or speeding citation, and prosecutors sometimes offer to reduce a reckless charge to a Class C offense. Either way, a reckless driving case should be reviewed by a lawyer before any plea.

Driving Safety Course and Deferred Disposition

For a qualifying moving violation, Code of Criminal Procedure article 45.0511 gives most drivers the right to have the charge dismissed by completing a TDLR-approved driving safety course. The request must be made on or before the appearance date, the driver must hold a valid Texas license and have liability insurance, the course can be used only once in a 12-month period, and it is not available to commercial driver's license holders or for speeding 25 mph or more over the limit or 95 mph or more. Our defensive driving page explains the El Paso process.

Deferred disposition under article 45.051 is the alternative. The court defers a finding of guilt for up to 180 days, sets conditions, and dismisses the case if they are met. It can be used more often than a driving safety course and is often the better choice when the driver has already used a course in the last year. It is likewise unavailable to CDL holders. Choosing between the two, or negotiating for a dismissal instead, is where our office earns its fee.

Frequently asked questions

Will a moving violation raise my car insurance in Texas?

A conviction for a moving violation reports to your DPS driving record, and most insurers review that record at renewal. Whether and how much a rate changes depends on the carrier, but keeping the conviction off the record through a dismissal, driving safety course, or deferred disposition is the most reliable way to avoid the question.

Can an expired registration ticket be dismissed?

Yes, by statute. Under Transportation Code §502.407 the court must dismiss the charge if the registration is renewed within 20 working days of the citation or before the first appearance, whichever is later, and an administrative fee of up to $20 is paid. Expired inspection charges have the same remedy under §548.605.

Is texting while driving a moving violation?

Yes. Transportation Code §545.4251 is a Class C misdemeanor committed while the vehicle is moving, and a conviction appears on the driving record. A driving safety course or deferred disposition may be available to keep it off the record.

I was cited for following too closely after a rear-end crash. Is that automatic?

No. The city must prove beyond a reasonable doubt that your following distance was unreasonable under the conditions. Officers who arrive after a collision usually did not see the distance, and that is a common basis for contesting the charge.

Can I take defensive driving if I have a CDL?

No. The driving safety course dismissal and deferred disposition are both unavailable to commercial driver's license holders, even for a ticket received in a personal vehicle. Our CDL page covers the options that remain.

Is reckless driving a Class C misdemeanor?

No. Reckless driving under §545.401 carries up to 30 days in county jail and is filed in County Court at Law, not Municipal Court. It should be handled as a criminal case, not paid as a ticket.
