Red Light & Stop Sign Ticket Lawyer El Paso, TX

Officer-issued red light and stop sign tickets turn on vantage point, signal timing and the stop line. We test that evidence before you plead.

Fight Your TicketCall 915-200-1133

A red light or stop sign ticket in El Paso, TX is written by a police officer, not a camera, and that changes how the case is defended. Texas outlawed photographic red-light enforcement in 2019 and El Paso has no red-light cameras, so every citation under Transportation Code §544.007 or §544.010 rests on what one officer says he or she saw from one position at one moment. Our office, led by attorney Robert Navar, defends these citations in El Paso Municipal Court, the El Paso County Justice of the Peace precincts, and the surrounding municipal courts in Socorro, Horizon City, Anthony, Vinton, Clint and Canutillo.

Disregarding a traffic signal or a stop sign is a Class C misdemeanor and a moving violation. Paying it is a plea of guilty or no contest, and the conviction goes on your driving record where insurers will find it. What §544.007 actually requires at a red or yellow light

Transportation Code §544.007 sets the rules for traffic-control signals. On a steady red, a driver must stop at the clearly marked stop line; if there is no stop line, before entering the crosswalk; and if there is no crosswalk, before entering the intersection. A right turn on red is permitted after a complete stop unless a sign prohibits it. A steady yellow is a warning that the green is ending, and a driver who enters the intersection on yellow has not violated the statute even if the light turns red while the vehicle is still clearing it.

Those details are where many cases are won. The question is not whether the light was red at some point, but whether the front of your vehicle crossed the stop line or entered the intersection after it turned red. A driver who was already past the line on yellow, or who stopped and then made a lawful right on red, has a defense even if the officer's description sounds damning.

Stop sign tickets under §544.010

Section 544.010 requires a driver approaching a stop sign to stop at the clearly marked stop line; if there is none, before entering the crosswalk on the near side; and if there is no crosswalk, at the point nearest the intersecting roadway where the driver has a view of approaching traffic. The driver must then yield to traffic that has the right-of-way before proceeding.

Most stop sign citations in El Paso are for a rolling stop. The dispute is usually about whether the wheels came to a complete rest and for how long. Texas law does not specify a number of seconds. An officer positioned behind you, or watching from a side street at a shallow angle, may genuinely be unable to tell a brief complete stop from a slow roll. We look at where the officer was, what was between the officer and your vehicle, and whether the stop line and sign at that intersection were placed and maintained the way the statute and the Texas Manual on Uniform Traffic Control Devices require.

Why the officer's vantage point is the heart of the case

Because there is no camera, the State's evidence is the officer's perception, and perception depends on geometry. An officer sitting at the cross street cannot see your signal head; he or she is inferring your light from the cross-traffic signal, which may be on a different phase or have an all-red clearance interval. An officer behind you sees your brake lights and the back of the signal, not the lens. An officer approaching from the opposite direction has to judge your position against the stop line from hundreds of feet away, often at night.

We ask for the officer's location, direction of travel, distance from the intersection, lighting conditions and any obstructions, and we compare that against the physical layout of the intersection. Where there is dash-camera or body-camera footage we request it; where there is none, the gaps in what the officer could actually see become the defense. Yellow timing and intersection evidence

Every signalized intersection in El Paso has a timing plan maintained by the City's traffic engineering staff. That plan specifies the yellow change interval and the all-red clearance interval for each approach, and those intervals are supposed to follow engineering standards tied to the approach speed. A yellow that is shorter than the standard for the posted speed creates what engineers call a dilemma zone, where a driver can neither stop safely nor clear the intersection before red. When the facts suggest a short yellow, we request the timing records and signal maintenance logs for the intersection and the date of the citation.

Other intersection evidence we gather includes the placement and visibility of the stop line and signal heads, construction or lane shifts in effect that day, sun angle for early-morning and late-afternoon citations, and whether a signal was in flash mode or malfunctioning.

Driving safety course and deferred disposition options

Running a red light or stop sign is a moving violation that is generally eligible for a driving safety course dismissal if you hold a valid Texas license, carry liability insurance, have not used the course in the last 12 months, and make the request on or before your appearance date. It is also generally eligible for deferred disposition under Code of Criminal Procedure art. 45A.301 (formerly art. 45.051), which defers a finding of guilt for up to 180 days and dismisses the case on successful completion. Neither option is available to commercial driver's license holders, who should read our CDL violation page.

A course or a deferral is sometimes the right answer. But each can be used only so often, and a conviction for disregarding a signal is one insurers treat seriously. When the officer's vantage point or the signal timing is questionable, we will tell you that a contested hearing is worth pursuing, and we will prepare for it. Red light and stop sign tickets after a collision

A significant share of these citations are issued after a crash, when the officer did not see the light at all and is reconstructing fault from statements and vehicle positions. The citation then becomes evidence in any insurance claim or civil case about the collision. Pleading guilty or no contest to the ticket can be used against you by the other driver's insurer. If you were cited after an accident, do not pay the ticket before talking to a lawyer. We defend the citation on its own terms and coordinate with whoever is handling the property-damage or injury side.

Frequently asked questions

Does El Paso have red light cameras?

No. Texas prohibited photographic traffic-signal enforcement statewide in 2019, and El Paso has no red-light cameras. Every red light citation in El Paso is issued by an officer based on personal observation, which is why vantage point and timing evidence matter so much.

I entered on yellow and it turned red while I was in the intersection. Did I break the law?

Under §544.007, a steady yellow is a warning that the green is ending. A driver who enters the intersection before the light turns red has not disregarded the signal. The dispute is usually about where your vehicle was at the instant of the change, and that is a fact question the State must prove.

How long do I have to stop at a stop sign in Texas?

The statute requires a complete stop at the stop line, crosswalk, or point where you can see cross traffic, but it does not set a number of seconds. A brief complete stop satisfies §544.010. Whether the wheels actually stopped is often a matter of what the officer could see from his or her position.

Can I take a driving safety course for a red light ticket?

In many cases, yes, if you hold a valid Texas license, have insurance, have not used the course in the past 12 months, and request it on or before your appearance date. CDL holders are not eligible. We will tell you whether the course or a contested hearing is the better path.

What is the fine for running a red light or stop sign in El Paso?

Both are Class C misdemeanors with a fine of up to $200 plus court costs. The conviction also goes on your driving record as a moving violation, which is usually the more expensive consequence through insurance.
