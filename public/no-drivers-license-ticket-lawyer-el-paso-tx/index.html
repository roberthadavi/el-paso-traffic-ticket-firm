No Driver's License Ticket Lawyer El Paso, TX

No license, failure to display and driving while license invalid (DWLI) tickets each have different penalties and defenses; several are dismissible with the right proof.

Fight Your TicketCall 915-200-1133

A no driver's license ticket lawyer in El Paso sees three very different charges hiding under one label. An officer can cite you for having no license at all, for failing to display a license you actually hold, or for driving while your license is invalid because of a suspension, revocation or expired status. The first two are Class C tickets, and one of them is dismissible with a single document. The third, driving while license invalid, can be a Class B misdemeanor with jail exposure depending on your history.

Our office, led by attorney Robert Navar, defends license cases in El Paso Municipal Court, the El Paso County Justice of the Peace precincts and the surrounding municipal courts. Because El Paso is a border city and a military city, a large share of these tickets involve Mexican licenses, out-of-state licenses and Fort Bliss soldiers whose licenses are perfectly valid under Texas law. The first thing we do is figure out which charge you are actually facing.

§521.021: operating a vehicle without a license

Transportation Code §521.021 requires anyone operating a motor vehicle on a Texas highway to hold a driver's license. A first offense is a Class C misdemeanor with a fine of up to $200, and the court adds state and local costs. Repeat convictions within a year carry escalating penalties, so the charge deserves attention even though the first ticket looks small.

The charge applies to someone who has never been licensed or whose license expired and was not renewed. It does not apply to a driver who holds a valid license from another state or country and is lawfully driving on it. If the officer wrote a no-license ticket when you actually hold a valid license, the case belongs under a different section, or under none at all, and we say so to the prosecutor.

§521.025: failure to display, dismissible with proof

Section 521.025 requires a driver to carry the license and display it on demand to a peace officer. Leaving your wallet at home is the classic example. The statute itself provides the remedy: the court may dismiss the charge if you produce a license that was valid at the time of the offense, and the court may charge a fee of up to $10 for the dismissal.

It still has to be handled by the appearance date. Drivers who pay online instead of presenting the license end up with a conviction for an offense that would have been dismissed for $10. We handle the dismissal request so the case closes correctly.

§521.457: driving while license invalid (DWLI)

Driving while license invalid under §521.457 covers driving when your license is suspended, revoked, cancelled or expired after a denial, or when you have been denied a license. It is the most serious of the three charges. The basic offense is a Class C misdemeanor, but the statute raises it to a Class B misdemeanor (up to 180 days in county jail and a fine of up to $2,000) when any of the following is true:

  • You have a prior DWLI conviction.
  • Your license was suspended because of a DWI conviction.
  • You did not have liability insurance at the time of the stop.

If the stop results in an accident with serious bodily injury or death, the charge becomes a Class A misdemeanor. A Class B or Class A DWLI is filed in county court, not in municipal or JP court, and it carries a criminal record that a Class C ticket does not. A DWLI conviction can also extend the suspension period that caused the problem in the first place. We review the DPS suspension history first, because the notice records are not always what the officer assumed.

How a conviction affects getting or keeping a license

A conviction under any of these sections goes on the DPS driving record. For someone who does not yet have a Texas license, that record follows them when they apply: a pending no-license case can prevent issuance until it is resolved, and an unpaid fine triggers an OmniBase hold that blocks DPS from issuing or renewing the license. For someone already suspended, a DWLI conviction may add time to the suspension and makes an occupational license harder to obtain.

A conviction also matters to insurance; a DWLI conviction in particular can lead to non-renewal or an SR-22 requirement. That is why we work toward dispositions that do not become convictions: dismissal on proof of a valid license, deferred disposition on a Class C charge, or a reduction where the facts support one. Paying the ticket is a plea of guilty or no contest and gives up all of those options.

Mexican licenses, foreign licenses and new Texas residents

Under Transportation Code §521.030, a nonresident who is at least 16 and holds a valid license from his or her home state or country may drive in Texas without a Texas license. A visitor from Juárez or Chihuahua with a valid Mexican license is lawfully driving in El Paso, and a no-license ticket written to that driver should not stand. The license has to be valid and the driver has to be a nonresident; a Texas resident who continues to drive on a Mexican license has a different problem.

Once a person becomes a Texas resident, §521.029 gives 90 days to obtain a Texas license. Officers and drivers often disagree about when residency began, and that question can decide the case. A Class C ticket in municipal or JP court is not an immigration proceeding, but if you have questions about how a court record could affect an immigration matter, we will refer you to immigration counsel rather than guess.

Fort Bliss soldiers and out-of-state licenses

Section 521.027 exempts a nonresident on active duty in the armed forces who holds a valid license from his or her home state, and extends the exemption to the service member's spouse and dependent children who hold home-state licenses. A soldier stationed at Fort Bliss driving on a valid Georgia or Ohio license does not need a Texas license, and a ticket written for no license should be dismissed on proof of the home-state license and military status.

The exemption does not cover a license that has expired or been suspended in the home state, and it does not cover a service member who has established Texas residency and let the home-state license lapse. We ask for the license, the orders and the dates so we can present the exemption correctly. If your original ticket was written by a DPS trooper, the case is in a county JP court; see our state trooper ticket page for how those courts work.

Frequently asked questions

I have a valid license but did not have it with me. Can the ticket be dismissed?

Usually yes. Transportation Code §521.025 allows the court to dismiss a failure-to-display charge when you show a license that was valid on the date of the stop, with a fee of up to $10. The request has to be made before the appearance date; paying the ticket online instead results in a conviction.

Is driving without a license a crime in Texas?

Driving without ever having been licensed, under §521.021, is a Class C misdemeanor with a fine of up to $200 on a first offense. It is a criminal offense, not a civil infraction, and a conviction goes on your DPS record, but it does not carry jail time on a first offense.

When is driving while license invalid a Class B misdemeanor?

Under §521.457, DWLI becomes a Class B misdemeanor if you have a prior DWLI conviction, if the suspension resulted from a DWI conviction, or if you had no liability insurance at the time of the stop. Class B carries up to 180 days in jail and a fine of up to $2,000 and is filed in county court.

I live in Juárez and drive with a Mexican license. Is that legal in El Paso?

Yes, if you are a nonresident of Texas, at least 16, and the Mexican license is valid. Section 521.030 recognizes a valid home-country license for visitors. A ticket for no driver's license written under those facts should be challenged, not paid.

I am a soldier at Fort Bliss with an out-of-state license. Do I need a Texas license?

No. Section 521.027 exempts active-duty nonresident service members, and their spouses and dependent children, who hold valid home-state licenses. Bring us the license and your orders and we will present the exemption to the court.

Will a no-license conviction keep me from getting a Texas license later?

An unresolved case or unpaid fine can trigger an OmniBase hold that blocks DPS from issuing a license. A conviction itself goes on your driving record and affects insurance. Resolving the case without a conviction, through dismissal or deferred disposition, avoids both problems.
