Animal Control Ticket Lawyer El Paso, TX

Dog at large, rabies vaccination, microchip, nuisance and tethering citations from El Paso Animal Services, handled in El Paso Municipal Court.

If you need an animal control ticket lawyer in El Paso, TX, our office handles the citations that El Paso Animal Services and El Paso police officers write under Title 7 of the El Paso Municipal Code. A dog that slipped out of the yard, a missed rabies booster, a neighbor's barking complaint, or a dog kept on a chain can each produce a Class C misdemeanor citation that is filed in El Paso Municipal Court and treated like any other criminal case there.

Many people assume an animal citation is a nuisance fee and pay it online. Paying is a plea of guilty or no contest, and it becomes a conviction on the court's record. That record is what Animal Services looks at when the next complaint comes in, and repeat convictions under Title 7 carry higher fines and can support a dangerous-dog or nuisance-animal finding. Attorney Robert Navar reviews the citation, the compliance paperwork, and the enforcement history before anyone enters a plea.

Common Title 7 Animal Citations in El Paso

Title 7 of the El Paso Municipal Code covers animal ownership inside the city limits. The citations we see most often from El Paso Animal Services include:

  • Dog or cat at large. An animal off the owner's property and not under restraint. This is the most common Title 7 citation and is frequently paired with a second charge if the animal lacked a current vaccination or microchip.
  • No current rabies vaccination. Required by Texas Health and Safety Code Chapter 826 and by the city code. A lapsed booster is enough to support the citation.
  • No microchip or registration. El Paso requires dogs and cats to be microchipped and the chip information kept current with the city.
  • Nuisance animal. Persistent barking, howling, odors, or an animal that repeatedly disturbs neighbors.
  • Unlawful restraint or tethering. Both the city code and Texas Health and Safety Code §821.077 restrict how a dog may be chained or tethered outdoors.
  • Dangerous dog or bite-related citations. Charges that follow a bite report or an attack on another animal, which can trigger a separate dangerous-dog determination.
  • Failure to confine a female in heat, excess animals, and sanitation violations.

What an Animal Services Citation Means Under Texas Law

An animal control citation is a Class C misdemeanor. It is prosecuted by the City of El Paso in El Paso Municipal Court, the fine is set by ordinance within the limits of Texas Local Government Code §54.001, and the case follows the Code of Criminal Procedure just like a traffic ticket. You have the right to plead not guilty, to see the complaint and the officer's report, and to a trial before a judge or jury.

Chapter 826 of the Health and Safety Code sets the rabies vaccination requirement statewide, and §826.022 makes failing to vaccinate a Class C misdemeanor on its own. The Legislature's Safe Outdoor Dogs Act added §821.077, which makes unlawful restraint of a dog a Class C misdemeanor and raises it to a Class B misdemeanor for a person who has a prior conviction under that section. Cruelty allegations are different: those fall under the Texas Penal Code, carry jail exposure, and are not Municipal Court matters. If an Animal Services investigation is heading in that direction, speak with a lawyer before giving any statement.

Proof-of-Compliance Dismissals

Many Title 7 citations exist to force compliance rather than to collect money. For a missing vaccination, microchip, or registration, the court and the city prosecutor will often consider dismissing the charge once the owner shows the problem has been fixed. Our office prepares that proof so it is in the right form when the case is called:

  • A current rabies certificate signed by a Texas-licensed veterinarian, with the tag number matching the animal described on the citation.
  • Microchip registration showing the chip number and the owner's current El Paso address and phone number.
  • Photographs and receipts for fence or gate repairs on an at-large citation.
  • Veterinary or shelter records showing the animal was sterilized, if that was a condition of release.

Compliance dismissals are discretionary. They are far more likely when the paperwork is complete, the owner has no recent Title 7 history, and the request is made before the case is set for trial. We will pursue a dismissal where the facts support it, and in cases where dismissal is not available, we will seek deferred disposition under Code of Criminal Procedure article 45.051 so the citation does not become a conviction.

Why Repeat Citations Are Treated Differently

Title 7 fines escalate with each conviction for the same owner, and Animal Services tracks history by owner name and address. A second at-large conviction inside a short window can push the case from a modest fine to a figure in the hundreds of dollars, and a pattern of complaints is often the foundation the city uses to seek a nuisance-animal order or to seize the animal. Keeping the first citation from becoming a conviction is the single most effective way to limit that exposure.

If you have an older animal citation you never resolved, the court may have issued a warrant and reported the case to OmniBase, which blocks your Texas driver's license renewal until the matter is cleared. Dangerous-Dog Determinations and Hearings

A bite report can lead to two separate proceedings. The first is a Class C citation in Municipal Court. The second is a dangerous-dog determination under Texas Health and Safety Code Chapter 822, Subchapter D, which is made by the animal control authority and can be appealed to a justice, county, or municipal court. A dangerous-dog finding is not a fine; it imposes ongoing obligations such as registration, secure enclosure, liability insurance or financial responsibility, and restraint rules, and a later incident involving a dog already declared dangerous carries serious criminal exposure for the owner.

The statute sets short deadlines for requesting a hearing and for appealing a determination. We review the bite report, witness statements, provocation evidence, and the animal's history, and we represent owners at the determination hearing and on appeal. Do not wait for the Municipal Court date to deal with the dangerous-dog notice; by then the appeal window may have closed.

Frequently asked questions

Is an El Paso Animal Services citation a criminal charge?

Yes. Title 7 violations are Class C misdemeanors filed in El Paso Municipal Court. Paying the citation is a plea of guilty or no contest and results in a conviction on the court's record.

Can a dog-at-large ticket be dismissed if I fix my fence and vaccinate the dog?

In many cases the city will consider dismissing or reducing a first-time citation once the owner shows proof of vaccination, microchipping, and a secure enclosure. The decision is discretionary, so the proof needs to be complete and presented before the case moves toward trial.

What happens if I ignore an animal citation?

Missing the appearance date can result in a warrant, an additional failure-to-appear charge, and an OmniBase hold on your Texas driver's license renewal. We can usually clear a warrant by posting an appearance bond and getting the case back on the docket.

Is tethering a dog illegal in El Paso?

Both the El Paso Municipal Code and Texas Health and Safety Code §821.077 restrict outdoor restraint. Chains, heavy tethers, improperly fitted collars, and restraint without shelter, shade, and water can be cited. A first offense is a Class C misdemeanor; a repeat offense under the state statute is a Class B misdemeanor.

My dog was declared dangerous. Can I appeal?

Yes. Texas Health and Safety Code Chapter 822 provides a hearing before the animal control authority and an appeal to a court, but the deadlines are short. Contact our office as soon as you receive the notice so the appeal is filed in time.

Do I have to go to court if I hire your office?

For most Title 7 citations we appear on your behalf at the initial settings. A trial or a dangerous-dog hearing may require your attendance, and we will tell you in advance if a date needs you there.
