What Happens If You Miss Your Court Date in El Paso Municipal Court?

Published November 1, 2026 · by Attorney Robert Navar

Missing a court date on a traffic ticket in El Paso Municipal Court is one of the most common ways a simple Class C citation turns into a real problem. Our office hears from drivers every week who forgot the date, never received a notice, moved, were deployed, or assumed the ticket would go away. It does not. Here is what happens after a missed appearance date and how an attorney gets the case back in front of a judge without you pleading guilty.

The appearance date on your citation is a legal promise

When an El Paso police officer hands you a citation, you sign it. That signature is not an admission of guilt. Under Texas Transportation Code §543.005, it is a written promise to appear in court on or before the date printed on the ticket. "Appear" does not mean you must stand in a courtroom. You can satisfy the promise by entering a plea in writing, requesting a court date, or hiring an attorney who files an appearance for you before the deadline.

If none of that happens by the appearance date, the promise is broken. From that point forward the court treats the case as a failure to appear, and a series of consequences begins to stack up.

The short window after the appearance date

El Paso Municipal Court does not usually issue a warrant the morning after the deadline. In practice the court sends a notice to the address on the citation and gives the driver a short grace period to respond. Code of Criminal Procedure article 45.014 requires the court to notify a defendant before an arrest warrant is issued for failing to appear on a citation. The notice goes to the address on the ticket, which is often an old address or a barracks address for a Fort Bliss soldier who has since moved. Many of our clients never saw it.

This window is the cheapest and simplest time to fix the case. If you realize you missed the date and the court has not yet issued a warrant, call us or use our traffic ticket and warrant lookup form and we will check the status of the citation and file an appearance before the situation escalates.

Failure to Appear becomes a second Class C charge

Once the window closes, the court typically files a new, separate charge against you for the failure itself. Texas law gives the court two statutes to do this. Transportation Code §543.009 makes it a misdemeanor to willfully violate a written promise to appear on a traffic citation. Penal Code §38.10, the general "bail jumping and failure to appear" statute, makes failing to appear a Class C misdemeanor when the underlying offense is punishable by fine only, which is true of every traffic ticket.

The practical effect is that your one speeding ticket is now two cases: the original citation and a Failure to Appear, sometimes labeled "VPTA" (Violate Promise to Appear) on the El Paso docket. Each carries its own fine of up to $500 plus court costs, and each is a separate criminal conviction if you are found guilty. In many cases the Failure to Appear charge ends up costing more than the ticket that started it.

The alias warrant, and later the capias pro fine

With the Failure to Appear charge filed, the judge signs an alias warrant for your arrest. "Alias" simply means it is a warrant issued after you failed to come to court on a pending case. It is a real arrest warrant. El Paso police officers see it on any traffic stop, and the El Paso County Sheriff and surrounding agencies can execute it. Our office has represented people arrested on old ticket warrants during a routine stop on I-10 and at their front door during a warrant roundup.

A second type of warrant comes into play later. If a case has already been resolved by a judgment, for example because you pleaded guilty or were found guilty but never paid, the court can issue a capias pro fine under Code of Criminal Procedure article 45.045. A capias pro fine is a warrant to bring you in because of an unpaid fine on a case that is already over. The distinction matters, because the defenses and the paperwork are different. An alias warrant means the case is still open and can still be fought. A capias pro fine means the case has ended and we are usually negotiating a payment arrangement, community service, or a hearing on your ability to pay.

OmniBase: the hold on your Texas driver license

The warrant is not the only consequence. El Paso Municipal Court participates in the Failure to Appear Program under Transportation Code Chapter 706, which is run by a vendor called OmniBase. When a case is reported to OmniBase, the Texas Department of Public Safety will refuse to renew your driver license until the court clears the hold. Once your license expires you cannot renew it, and driving on an expired license is a separate citation. Clearing the hold requires resolving the case with the court and paying a reimbursement fee for each reported case.

Scofflaw: the hold on your vehicle registration

Transportation Code §502.010 lets a county refuse to register a vehicle when the owner has an outstanding warrant for failure to appear or failure to pay a fine on a traffic complaint. El Paso County uses this "scofflaw" program. The effect is that when your registration sticker comes due, the El Paso County Tax Office will deny the renewal until the court reports the case as resolved. Expired registration is then another ticket, and the cycle repeats.

Do not just pay the ticket online

When people discover a warrant, the first instinct is to go to the court's website and pay whatever it shows. We strongly recommend against it. Paying a traffic citation in Texas is a plea of guilty or no contest. It is a conviction. For a moving violation, that conviction goes on your DPS driving record for years, your insurer can see it, and if you hold a commercial driver license it can affect your livelihood. Paying also does nothing to contest the Failure to Appear charge, so you may end up with two convictions instead of one. Once a plea is entered and the fine is paid, the options we would normally use, such as deferred disposition or a driving safety course dismissal, are usually gone.

How an attorney gets the case back on the docket

This is the part of the process that an experienced El Paso warrant lawyer handles routinely. Code of Criminal Procedure article 45.016 allows a defendant to post a bail bond or cash bond to secure an appearance. In El Paso Municipal Court, an attorney can file a notice of appearance along with an appearance bond on each open case. When the court accepts the bond, it recalls the warrant, lifts the OmniBase and scofflaw reporting once the case is set, and places the original citation and the Failure to Appear charge back on the docket for a hearing. You are not arrested, and in most cases you do not need to appear personally until the case is actually heard.

From there we treat the case like any other citation. We review whether the stop and the citation were proper and pursue the best available outcome, which may be a dismissal, deferred disposition, a driving safety course, or a reduced plea to a non-moving violation. We also address the Failure to Appear charge directly. Judges have discretion to dismiss or reduce a VPTA charge when the driver had a legitimate reason for missing the date, such as a deployment, a hospitalization, or a notice sent to the wrong address, and we will present that explanation on your behalf.

If you have more than one old ticket

Old tickets travel in groups. A driver who missed one date in Municipal Court often has an unresolved ticket from a Justice of the Peace precinct, Socorro, Horizon City, or Anthony as well, each with its own warrant and hold. We check every El Paso-area court and post bonds in each one. Frequently asked questions

How long after the appearance date does El Paso Municipal Court issue a warrant?

There is no fixed number of days. The court generally mails a notice to the address on the citation and gives a short period to respond before it files a Failure to Appear charge and issues an alias warrant. Because the notice often goes to an outdated address, we recommend checking the case status as soon as you realize the date has passed rather than waiting for mail.

Will I be arrested if I walk into the courthouse to fix my warrant?

Walking in without a bond carries a real risk of being taken into custody on the warrant, and you will usually be asked to enter a plea on the spot. Having an attorney post an appearance bond first allows the warrant to be recalled and the case reset without an arrest, and without pleading guilty.

What is the difference between an alias warrant and a capias pro fine?

An alias warrant is issued when you miss a court date on a case that is still open, which means the case can still be contested. A capias pro fine is issued after a judgment when a fine has gone unpaid, which means the case is over and the issue is collecting or working out the fine. The steps to resolve each are different.

Can the Failure to Appear charge itself be dismissed?

In many cases, yes. Judges have discretion to dismiss or reduce a Failure to Appear or VPTA charge, particularly when the driver had a legitimate reason for missing the date or never received the notice. We cannot promise a particular outcome, but we raise this on every case where the facts support it.
